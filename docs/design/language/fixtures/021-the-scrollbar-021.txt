a block on a hairline is two different things in one column. A light
line and a heavy line are one thing at two weights — which is what a
track and a thumb ARE.

★ and ╽ ╿ give HALF-ROW precision, with the track running through them

  scroll   0 of 28   ┃ ┃ ┃ ╿ │ │ │ │ │ │ │ │
  scroll   5 of 28   │ ╽ ┃ ┃ ┃ │ │ │ │ │ │ │
  scroll  14 of 28   │ │ │ │ ┃ ┃ ┃ ╿ │ │ │ │
  scroll  28 of 28   │ │ │ │ │ │ │ │ ╽ ┃ ┃ ┃

  a twelve-row gutter has TWENTY-FOUR positions. Without it a bar over
  a long document moves in visible jumps and sits still through half
  a page of scrolling — which reads as broken rather than coarse.

  it is the same trick the plot renderer takes for a half-block — the
  cell is the unit the terminal gives you, and the glyph is how you
  get underneath it.

the thumb takes the ACCENT when its container has focus

  ││╽┃┃╿││    muted    scrolling under a focused prompt
  ││╽┃┃╿││    accent   you are IN it — the arrows move here
  the same rule the focused-container border takes, and it costs no
  extra column.

★ FOCUS PULLS THE VIEWPORT. Scrolling never moves focus.

  the two directions are not symmetric, and treating them as if they
  were is the commonest scrolling bug in a TUI:

  move focus past the edge   the view follows, by the MINIMUM
  scroll away from focus     focus STAYS, and it is still there
                             when you come back

  one row past the edge scrolls ONE ROW, not half a screen — re-
  centring on every step makes the text jump under a reader who is
  trying to follow it.

where a bar appears

  the transcript      whenever it overflows
  a scroll block      inside its own bounds, not the frame's
  another agent's tab its own; the other tab's is not drawn
  an overlay          only if it overflows — usually it does not
  nothing fits        NOTHING. A bar that cannot move is decoration

  two bars is legal when they are two documents. Two bars for one
  document is a layout error.
