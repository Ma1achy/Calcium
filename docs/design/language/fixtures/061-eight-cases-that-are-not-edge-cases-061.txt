eight cases that look like edge cases and are not — every one is
something a reader hits by accident on the first afternoon.

1 · ORDINARY CONTENT ARRIVES. Three states, three answers.
  at the bottom     follow it. You were reading the end
  scrolled up       DO NOT MOVE. The anchor holds, and the chrome
                    counts what arrived — a button, above the prompt
  navigating        DO NOT MOVE. Focus is a position and a new
                    entry is not a reason to leave it
  an ownership-request event is different: a new question explicitly
  raises its owner and arms it against in-flight input.

  one of these three is wrong in most terminal apps, and it is
  always the second: the view yanks to the bottom while you read.

2 · TYPING WHILE A CHOICE-ONLY QUESTION IS OPEN
  the prompt is held, so a letter has nowhere to land. But a SILENT
  no-op reads as a frozen app.

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 ⟩ Apply this change?     ▲ answer this first
  › no    yes    show full diff
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
←→ move     ⏎ answer   — esc resolves to NO

  the first keystroke that is not an answer puts that notice on the
  question, once. It is not an error and it does not repeat.

3 · PASTE, IN FOUR PLACES
  the prompt, small    inserted at the caret, as text
  the prompt, large    a chip — [#1 json · 47L] — at the caret
  a text question      into its isolated editor; implementation shared, state separate
  not focused          focus the prompt first, then paste. A paste is
                       a request to type, so it goes where typing goes

  and a paste is NEVER submitted by its own newlines — bracketed
  paste exists precisely so a pasted \n is a newline and not a send.

4 · ⌃C INSIDE A SUBAGENT CANCELS THAT AGENT
  not all of them, and not the parent. You are looking at one, and
  the frame you are looking at is the one your keys act on.

  cancelling the whole fan-out is ⌃C on the PARENT's entry, where
  the head says 3 agents — which is a different place, deliberately.

5 · A GRAPHEME IS ONE THING TO THE CARET AND TWO CELLS TO THE SCREEN
  ❯ ship it ⚡ now
  ←→ move ONE index and the caret jumps TWO columns. ⌫ removes the
  whole cluster — never half an emoji, never a lone combining mark.

  the same rule the span model takes, one layer down: the unit is
  the cluster and the WIDTH is a separate question.

6 · THE WINDOW LOSES FOCUS
  held keys stop repeating. The cursor stops blinking. Spinners and
  ramps keep going, because the work has not stopped.

  a held arrow that keeps repeating after you alt-tab away is a
  scroll position you did not choose, and the release event never
  arrives to end it.

7 · A FOCUSED BLOCK IS REPLACED OR EVICTED WHILE YOU ARE IN IT
  a streaming block is replaced whole on every patch, and the
  transcript evicts at its cap. Focus resolves BY ID and falls
  forward to the nearest survivor — the same operation a resize and
  focus memory both use.

  and if the whole ENTRY went, focus lands on its neighbour rather
  than at the prompt. Being moved is survivable; being ejected is
  not.

8 · THE CONNECTION DROPS MID-TURN
  ● The parser tracks quotes with a boolean, so a nes

  ─────────  ERROR  ─────────
  ✗ transport closed after 312 tokens

  ✦ retrying in 8s · attempt 2     2 queued, held

  the partial reply STAYS. The queue is HELD rather than dropped or
  sent — and it says so, because a queue that silently fired on
  reconnect is the worst outcome of the three.

the thread through all eight

  nothing moves the reader without their asking, and no accepted or rejected
  command silently does nothing.

  those are the two failures people describe as the app feeling
  broken, and neither of them is a bug in the usual sense.
