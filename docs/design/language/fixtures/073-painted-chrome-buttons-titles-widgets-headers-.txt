the error tag is the only painted thing that means a STATUS. Everything
below is painted because it is a SURFACE — a thing you act on or a thing
that holds other things. Different job, no confusion.

buttons — four states, and the ground is the affordance

   Approve    Deny         resting
   › Approve    Deny       focused — ⏎ activates
   Approve    Deny         a destructive pair takes tone:
   Approve    Deny         green to go, red to stop
   Approve    Deny         disabled — a WELL, not a button
  [ Approve ]  [ Deny ]       1-bit and ascii — brackets carry it

titles — three treatments, and each says something different

   workers ──────────────────────────────────────     painted — a NAMED region
  ┌ containers ────────────────────────────────────┐   inset — the default, cheapest
  containers  ──────────────────────────────────────       bare — a heading, not a container

   running  3 ──────────────────────────────────────   a title that carries a STATE
   degraded  1 ────────────────────────────────────  and a count beside it

  it is a SURFACE the rows sit under, not a status — and it makes the
  column names stop competing with the data for the eye.

what must never be painted

  a status word          running  not  running
  a permission posture   » auto   a posture is text
  body text              anything you read at length
  a whole block          a wash over content is a MODE, and there is none

  the test: am I painting a THING (a button, a track, a header) or a
  FACT about a thing (its status, its mode, its outcome)?
  Things take a ground. Facts take a tone.
