four principles, and the third is the one that decides the rest

1 · USE THE KEYS PEOPLE ALREADY KNOW [R-KEY-003]
  arrows move. ⏎ confirms. esc backs out. ⇥ moves focus. ⇧+arrow
  selects. ⌃⇧C copies. Every GUI on earth agrees, and a terminal
  app disagreeing with all of them is a terminal app people avoid.

2 · SCOPES, NOT MODES [R-INT-001]
  a mode is something you ENTER and forget you are in. A scope is
  WHERE FOCUS IS, and focus is drawn. It shows the candidate navigation
  keyset. OWNERSHIP separately decides which scope may handle an event;
  being focused does not by itself make a scope the keyboard owner.

3 · NO SINGLE-KEY COMMANDS WHERE TYPING IS POSSIBLE [R-INT-002]
  y to copy, ⌘↓ to the bottom, n and p to move — three traditions,
  none of them shared, all of them invisible. A letter key types a
  letter; in any owner that accepts text it never does something.

  this is the expensive one and it is worth it: it means you can
  type wherever text is accepted, without checking what has focus.

  the prompt is protected by the rule. This resolved profile also keeps
  letters out of the TRANSCRIPT by convention and PROXIMITY: the
  transcript is one esc from the prompt and a reader moves between
  them constantly, so a letter there is a letter they will type by
  accident. A captured block is somewhere you went deliberately.

  so: inside a
  block, 1–9 toggling a plot's series and r resetting a camera are
  legal. In the transcript they are not.

4 · NOTHING HIDDEN [R-KEY-004]
  at ordinary rungs the footer shows OWNER, primary action, safe exit and HELP.
  Constrained rungs retain owner plus the highest-ranked reachable safe action;
  the full resolved set remains reachable through help or its named command.
  A binding nobody can discover is a binding that does not exist.
