C16 and C26 were written before this pass. Where they disagree with it,
here is which wins and why.

the specs are the authority on MECHANISM. This document is the authority
on SHAPE. A binding is a shape; the store it writes is a mechanism.

what changed, and every one is a binding rather than a behaviour

  newline          ⌥⏎  →  ⇧⏎      what people type, and the
                                   protocol makes it detectable
  copy             y     →  ⌃⇧C     a letter types a letter
  bottom           G     →  ⌘↓       it was never a mode
  find             ⌃r    →  ⌃⇧F     find, as everywhere else
  scroll           ⇞ ⇟   →  ⌥↑↓     ⇞ ⇟ are not on a laptop
  move by entry    n p   →  ↑↓       they were always arrows
  leave the prompt —     →  ⇧⇥      shift-tab moves focus back
  switch agent     —     →  ⌃⇥      agents are tabs

  none of these changes what the key DOES — the action, the store
  and the frame are the specs'. Only the chord moved.

what this pass ADDS that the specs have no row for

  focusGround         a lightness ground for the cursor. C10 has
                      selection and no focus surface
  keyboardProtocol    C02's tenth capability field. It is
                      UNCONSIDERED there, not deferred
  per-binding repeat  DAS and ARR as a decision, which needs the
                      protocol's release events to exist at all
  the scope footer    the last line naming the live keys

★ and one RULE needed refining, because the legend caught it

  I wrote NO SINGLE-LETTER COMMANDS ON CONTENT. But the gesture
  table has 1–9 toggling a plot's series, which is exactly that.

  the rule's REASON was: while an editor owns input, ordinary text keys
  keep typing there without a focus check. The rule bites wherever typing
  is possible; non-typing owners may declare single-key actions.

  corrected: NO SINGLE-KEY COMMANDS IN ANY SCOPE WHERE TYPING IS
  POSSIBLE. Inside a block nothing is typed, so 1–9 on a legend and
  r to reset a camera are legal and good.

  which is the shape of correction this whole exercise keeps
  producing: the rule was right and its statement was too wide.

what the specs got right and this document IMPORTED unchanged

  click again is a state test, not a timing one
  focus memory, resolved by id, falling forward
  hover may preview inline targets or move a plot readout; never moves focus
  or commits domain/action state
  press arms; release commits only to the same stable identity
  right-click has no key equal, so it has no effect
  a click on a non-element row is unconsumed
  a selection cannot straddle two entries
  the wheel acts on the box under the POINTER, not the focused one

  all eight are better than anything I would have arrived at, and
  six of them are answers to questions I had not thought to ask.

what to do when they conflict during the build

  1  a CHORD conflict — this document wins, and the spec's row is
     updated rather than exempted
  2  a MECHANISM conflict — the spec wins, and this document is
     wrong about how something works
  3  a RULE conflict — neither wins automatically. Find the rule's
     reason and check whether it reaches the case, as the legend
     did above

  and the third is the common one, which is why a rule in this
  document without its reason beside it is a rule that will be
  applied somewhere it does not belong.
