six rulings the gesture table settles, and none of them is obvious

1 · FOCUS-FIRST ACTIVATION IS A STATE TEST, NOT A TIMING ONE [R-PTR-001]
  there is no double-click speed. On a FOCUS-FIRST target the first click
  focuses; a click on the ALREADY-FOCUSED element activates it — whether
  that is 200ms later or a minute. Direct-action controls activate on
  their first committed release and have no focus-first precondition.

  so a slow double-click works, a shaky hand works, and there is no
  number to tune. The framework reads no clock for this, which is
  also why it cannot have a timing test even if it wanted one.

2 · FOCUS IS REMEMBERED, AND RESTORING IS A RE-RESOLUTION [R-PTR-002]
  leave a table on row 14, come back, and you are on row 14. There
  is no click-to-jump-back in a terminal, so re-entering at row 1
  every time is punishing rather than merely untidy.

  but the element may be GONE — the transcript evicts, a block is
  replaced whole, and the element list is a function of width. So
  it resolves by id and FALLS FORWARD to the nearest survivor,
  which is the scroll anchor's shape reused rather than a second
  mechanism.

3 · HOVER MAY PREVIEW; A PLOT MAY MOVE ITS READOUT [R-PTR-003]
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 4.0┤⣀⠡⠤⠒│⠉⠑⠒⠢⡄
  │ 0.0┼──────────┬──────────────────
  │       step 41  ·  loss 0.0372
  └──────────────────────────────────────┘

  the readout follows the pointer and FOCUS DOES NOT MOVE. It passes
  the every-gesture-has-a-key rule because ←→ set the same store —
  this is the readout half of that pair without the focus half.

  leaving the area LEAVES THE CURSOR WHERE IT WAS. A readout that
  vanished with the pointer would have no keyboard equal.

  and it is consumed only when the INDEX changes — a motion inside
  one sample's columns is a no-op and no frame, which is what makes
  tracking every motion affordable at all.

  links, buttons and chips may preview their focus treatment. Other
  surfaces do nothing. Hover never moves focus, activates, or commits domain/action
  state; a component may update its declared preview or readout state.

4 · CLICKING A LEGEND ENTRY TOGGLES THAT SERIES [R-PTR-004]
  ● train   ● val   ○ test        click a swatch or its label
  the key equal is ↓ then 1–9. A legend hit TOGGLES on its first committed
  click even when the plot was unfocused, and may also focus the plot; the
  plot background remains focus-first. A legend entry is a control, so
  it does its own thing rather than the generic one.

5 · PRESS ARMS; RELEASE COMMITS BY IDENTITY [R-PTR-005]
  the press records the target's stable id and ownership generation.
  release activates only over that same live id. A drag, owner change,
  replacement or release elsewhere cancels it, so a drag never ends in a click.

6 · RIGHT-CLICK HAS NO KEY EQUAL, SO IT HAS NO EFFECT [R-PTR-006]
  recorded rather than absorbed: a right-click needs a keyboard
  equal BEFORE it can have a behaviour, and there is no ⇧↓-shaped
  state for it to reach.

  same for ⌘ and ⌃ clicks — which is also what leaves ⌘click to the
  terminal, where a reader already expects it.

and the one the table refuses

  a click on a row that is NO element — chrome, a gap, blank space
  beside a short block — does nothing and is unconsumed.

  “no element” is a state the keys never produce and the frame never
  highlights, so a click that stored it would change state INVISIBLY.
  A row with nothing focusable is not entered.
