a resize is not a redraw — three things have to survive it

  the SCROLL   by anchor: the top visible entry stays the top
               visible entry, whatever its new height is
  the FOCUS    by id, falling forward to the nearest survivor —
               the element list is a function of width, so the
               element you were on may not exist at 60 columns
  the CARET    by character index, not by column — a rewrapped
               line puts the same character somewhere else

  none of the three is a position on screen. All three are
  identities, and the position is recomputed — which is why a resize
  does not feel like losing your place.

what a resize is allowed to change

  how much is visible, the wrapping, a plot's sample count, a
  table's columns, whether the label on the prompt's rule fits.

what it is NOT allowed to change

  the scope. A resize while you are inside a block leaves you inside
  it — and if the block lost its rows entirely, you land on the block
  rather than at the prompt.

  a resize that dumps you back to the prompt is a resize that made
  you start again.

and it coalesces

  a drag is thirty events. One frame at the end of the window, not
  thirty — which is measured, not assumed: this tree once spent
  544ms on a 30-event drag before the window was added.
