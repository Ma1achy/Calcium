the rework, in one sentence: a Prism command is a CALL.

BEFORE — every command produced a bespoke framed panel with its own
gutter, its own rules, its own header, its own footer hints.
  ▌ ── ps · 4 of 11 · --mine · last 24h ───────────────────
  ▌
  ▌   uuid     family          status
  ▌ ▸ a3f9b21  digit-classif…  running
  ▌
  ▌ ⏎ detail  ␣ expand  ≡ logs

AFTER — the call grammar, which every surface already uses
  ● ps --mine --since 24h · 0.3s · 4 of 11
    ⎿  uuid     family            status      metric   age
       a3f9b21  digit-classifier  running     0.0372    23m
       7c2d4e1  decoder-zoom      succeeded   0.0089    41m
       2e8a04c  graphsage         failed           —   1h12m
       f410d99  flow-predictor    queued            —      3m

what that one change buys

  the ▌ gutter, the panel rules, the per-surface footer hint and the
  bespoke header are all GONE — four pieces of chrome per surface,
  across fifteen surfaces.

  the result-summary slot now says 4 of 11 where the old header said it,
  and it says it in the same place every other call does.

  the table is bounded and windowed like any block, so a 400-run
  list is eight rows and a residue — which the panel version had to
  solve for itself.

  and the keys are the FRAMEWORK's: ⇥ to it, ↑↓ the rows, ⏎ opens,
  ⌃⇧C copies the values. No surface teaches its own.

  ▲ and it makes Prism and the agent ONE product rather than two
  sharing a renderer — a /ps result and a read_file result are the
  same object with different contents, which is what §1 claimed all
  along and the old surfaces quietly contradicted.
