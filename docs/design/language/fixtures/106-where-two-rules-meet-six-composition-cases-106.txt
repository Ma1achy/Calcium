WHERE TWO RULES MEET — the cases neither rule answers alone

individual rules are well specified. The document is thin exactly where
two of them meet, and that is where interaction models break.

1 · A QUESTION ARRIVES WHILE A PANEL IS OPEN

  a panel is DISMISSABLE and a question is NOT. Two layers, and one of
  them is about to take the keyboard.

  THE PANEL CLOSES FIRST, and its state is held with the prompt’s.
  A panel is a thing you opened; a question is a thing that arrived. The
  arriving one cannot silently sit under something you were reading, and
  stacking two dismissable-versus-not layers makes esc ambiguous.

  and the question still takes the activation guard described in INPUT
  OWNERSHIP — so the ⏎ that would have taken a completion does not answer it.

2 · A RESIZE WHILE INSIDE A PLOT, WITH A MODAL PENDING

  three rules want the same moment: a resize keeps your SCOPE (§59), a
  modal OWNS input (§102), and focus falls forward by id (§103).

  THE LADDER DECIDES, because it always does. The modal owns the
  keyboard, so the resize changes what is DRAWN and not who OWNS it — the
  plot re-solves underneath, the camera is kept, and when the question
  resolves you are back inside the plot exactly where you were.

  ★ a resize is a RENDER event. It can never change the OWNER — which is
  the same reason §59 says it cannot change the scope.

3 · TWO QUESTIONS, AND THE FIRST IS BEING INSPECTED

  §97 says an inspection SUSPENDS a question. The queue rule says a second
  question WAITS. What does the second one do while the first is
  suspended and therefore not on screen?

  IT WAITS, and the inspection is not a resolution. The queue is of
  UNRESOLVED questions, and suspended is unresolved — which is the
  whole point of the word. Leaving the inspection returns to the first
  question, not to the second.

  EVERY QUESTION RESOLVES EXACTLY ONCE, and a suspension is not one.

4 · A WATCHED RUN FINISHES WHILE ITS ENTRY IS EVICTED

  the visible window evicts at a cap. A watch retains a stable EVENT ID
  in the durable record, so it can outlive that event's current rendering.

  ⏎ REHYDRATES THE WATCHED EVENT, preserving its identity.
  Completion is a new durable event linked to that watched ID, so it
  appears at the end without pretending to replace history. The watch
  can open either fact; eviction changed only the window.

5 · THE CONNECTION DROPS WHILE A QUESTION IS OPEN

  the child that asked is gone. The question is still on screen and
  still owns the keyboard.

  IT RESOLVES ITSELF, VISIBLY — as EXPIRED, not as denied.

  ○ the question expired ·  seams  disconnected after 41s

  denied would be a lie about what happened, and leaving it open would
  hold the keyboard for an owner that no longer exists. Expiry is the
  third resolution, and it is why §97 says CANCELLATION AND EXPIRY
  INCLUDED rather than just answered.

6 · A SORTED TABLE REORDERS UNDER A HELD POINTER

  a live table patches while you are pressing on a row. The row at
  those coordinates on RELEASE is a different row.

  THE GESTURE IS BOUND TO THE ELEMENT’S IDENTITY, not the coordinates.
  Press ARMS the element it landed on; release COMMITS only if the
  pointer is still over THAT ELEMENT. Otherwise nothing happens, and
  nothing happening is correct — it is the pointer’s version of §97’s
  rule about events assigned to one owner.

★ the thread through all six

  EVERY ONE IS ANSWERED BY A RULE THAT ALREADY EXISTS, applied to a
  case it was not written for. None of them needed a new mechanism —
  which is the test for whether the rules are the right ones.

  and the one thing they share: A RESIZE, A PATCH AND AN ORDINARY CONTENT
  ARRIVAL ARE RENDER EVENTS. They change what is drawn; they never change
  the owner. An ownership-request event declares that change explicitly.
